Candidate Count Model Research: Comprehensive List of Unresolved Questions
Cross-Repository Extraction for Rune-Factory-Inheritance-Research

Created: 2026-08-01
Revised: 2026-08-01 (incorporates the 50-trial RF4SP Pink Turnip follow-up)

Purpose
-------
This document collects unresolved questions concerning the Candidate Count Model and related research from Markdown, TXT, PDF, CSV, appendices, observation logs, and Google Drive research materials.

It does not claim to establish the game's internal implementation. It distinguishes questions explicitly identified as unresolved from validation tasks that remain logically open based on existing observations.

Status Labels
-------------
[Explicit]  Explicitly described in the source material as unresolved, untested, unknown, or hypothetical.
[Boundary]  Observed, but its range of validity or generalizability has not been established.
[Derived]   Arises from integrating multiple sources; it is not a direct claim made by any one source.
[On Hold]   Interpretation is deferred because experimental controls or records are insufficient.

Important Premises
------------------
- The Candidate Count Model is a replaceable approximation for organizing observations from actual gameplay, not proof of internal code.
- The 4,000 trials strongly support consistency between the model and the three- through six-stack conditions tested for Messhilite inheritance in RF5.
- Those trials directly tested success rates under specific conditions, not the full internal processes for candidate generation, weighting, order, or recursion.
- Under an RF4SP candidate-overflow condition, combinations excluding Pink Turnip occurred in 42 of 50 trials. The reproduced bias is difficult to explain as equal-probability selection among the four combinations.
- This is a potential counterexample to the auxiliary assumption that candidate subsets are always selected with equal probability. It neither falsifies the Candidate Count Model as a whole nor proves an internal weighted-selection implementation.
- CSV data supplies observations and aggregate values, but does not directly identify internal algorithms.

======================================================================
A. Defining the Candidate Set Itself
======================================================================

A-01 [Explicit] What counts as one candidate
It remains unclear whether the equipment itself, the source equipment, internal arrangement materials, required materials, auto-arranged materials, and source-contamination elements should all be counted as equivalent single candidates.

A-02 [Explicit] Whether the candidate counts for shoes and accessories are 4/5/6 or 5/6/7
The count changes depending on whether source contamination is treated as an independent candidate. This must be reconsidered separately from the directional claim that more candidates reduce the success rate.

A-03 [Explicit] Whether the source equipment itself and its internal arrangements are separate candidates
Both may become candidates simultaneously, but the identifying conditions and duplication relationship are unresolved.

A-04 [Explicit] How multiple copies of the same material are represented
They may be independent candidates, a same-type population, or a compressed candidate. Messhilite success rates changed with the number of same-type inputs, but the internal representation is unknown.

A-05 [Explicit] Duplicate handling for candidates with the same name or type
It is unknown whether duplicates remain distinct, are compressed as equivalent candidates, or are merged only under certain conditions.

A-06 [Explicit] Whether candidate compression exists and when it occurs
Some cases change less than expected as apparent candidate count increases. Compression, fixed slots, and priority have not yet been distinguished.

A-07 [Explicit] Candidate-exclusion conditions
What is excluded from the final candidate set, and at which processing stage, remains unknown.

A-08 [Explicit] Effective upper limit on candidate count
The effective maximum after accounting for crafting input slots, three arrangement slots, recursive expansion, and source contamination is unknown.

A-09 [Derived] Whether candidate presence and eligibility for selection are equivalent
If fixed slots or internal priority exist, mere presence does not express the effective candidate count. An effective or weighted N may be required instead of a simple N.

A-10 [Derived] Whether the candidate set is built once or reconstructed at each stage
A multistage structure—generation, compression or priority processing, then final three-slot selection—remains possible.

Primary evidence: PDFs 04, 05, 06, 07, 11, and 12; 99_補遺_追加未解決事項備忘録.txt

B. Candidate-Generation Paths
======================================================================

B-01 [Explicit] Exact conditions under which directly added materials become candidates
Differences by equipment type, input position, source-equipment role, and additional-material role remain unresolved.

B-02 [Explicit] Exact conditions under which required recipe materials become candidates
The auto-arrange hypothesis for recipes requiring more than three materials exists, but has not been confirmed across all recipes and equipment types.

B-03 [Explicit] Whether only source equipment receives special reference treatment
Internal information from source equipment appears easier to reference, while recursive expansion from additional equipment appears weak or absent. Whether position or role causes this is unknown.

B-04 [Explicit] Conditions for expanding internal arrangements from additionally added equipment
It is unclear whether they are never expanded or merely were not expanded under the tested conditions.

B-05 [Explicit] Conditions under which auto-arrange information becomes a candidate again downstream
It is likely retained internally in completed equipment, but the relationship between retention and later expansion is unresolved.

B-06 [Explicit] Conditions for source contamination
It is observed in shoes and accessories. Its presence in weapons, armor, shields, and other categories—and its dependence on equipment category or stat-transfer processing—remains unsettled.

B-07 [Explicit] Relationship between inheritance depth and candidate growth
Candidates tend to accumulate through repeated inheritance, but the number added per generation has not been established.

B-08 [Derived] Treatment of the same candidate generated through multiple paths
If the same named element appears as a required material, internal arrangement, and source-contamination element, it may be duplicated, compressed, or given greater weight.

B-09 [Derived] Timing of candidate generation
Candidates may be generated when crafting begins, when the source is established, during auto-arrange processing, or immediately before final three-slot selection.

B-10 [Derived] Relationship between candidate generation and displayed information
It is unclear how many undisplayed internal candidates are included in inferences based on blue text, descriptions, and completed stats.

Primary evidence: PDFs 03, 04, 05, and 06; Auto-Arrange.md; Recursive-Processing.md; Self-Contamination.md

C. Final-Selection Algorithm and Weighted Selection
======================================================================

C-01 [Explicit] Whether selection is completely uniform
The model's basic formula assumes uniform selection without replacement, but this has not been proven as the internal implementation.

C-02 [Explicit] Whether weighted selection exists
Unusually frequent or rare candidates, retention of existing arrangements, and displacement of auto-arranged candidates motivate this question.

C-03 [Explicit] What determines each candidate's weight
Candidate type, input order, existing arrangements, source role, equipment category, duplicate count, generation, and game-version differences have not been separated.

C-04 [Explicit] Whether internal priority exists
Existing arrangements or source-derived candidates may outrank auto-arranged candidates, but the evidence is weak and includes single-trial observations.

C-05 [Explicit] Whether fixed slots exist
Frequently retained candidates may be highly weighted selections or fixed slots outside the draw; current observations do not distinguish them.

C-06 [Explicit] Whether selection occurs in stages
Possible processes include category selection followed by candidate selection, or retention of existing slots followed by filling vacancies.

C-07 [Explicit] Number of internal draws
It is unknown whether one three-candidate combination is drawn, candidates are drawn sequentially, or invalid candidates trigger redraws.

C-08 [Explicit] Whether overflow triggers redraw, reconstruction, or shuffling
Changes in displayed order alone cannot uniquely identify the internal event.

C-09 [Explicit] Whether selection is without replacement
The approximation assumes this, but it remains unproven, especially when multiple same-type inputs may remain individually identifiable.

C-10 [Explicit] Whether category-level priority exists
Equipment bodies, materials, existing arrangements, and auto-arranged materials may receive category-level priority.

C-11 [Boundary] Conditions producing different outcome distributions at the same N
If composition or generation path changes behavior while N stays constant, the limits of an N-only model must be identified.

C-12 [Derived] Whether weighted selection and candidate compression can be distinguished statistically
Apparent bias can be explained by weighting, fixed slots, duplicate compression, or staged selection.

C-13 [Explicit] RNG seed, random-number consumption, and selection timing
Seed updates, changes after loading, and RNG consumption between crafting actions are unknown.

C-14 [Derived] Whether repeated save/load trials can be treated as independent
The 4,000-trial analysis treats them as independent in practice, but independence of the internal seed sequence was not directly verified.

C-15 [Explicit] The RF4SP “Pink Turnip problem” under candidate overflow
In 50 overflow trials, combinations excluding Pink Turnip occurred 42 times (84%). The bias first observed in 30 trials remained at nearly the same level after 20 additional trials. Against equal probability among four combinations, χ²=93.84, df=3, and Cramér's V≈0.791. Simple uniform selection is therefore difficult to reconcile with this outcome distribution.

C-16 [Explicit] Cause of the Pink Turnip bias
Possible causes include material-specific weight, item ID or registration order, input order, candidate-generation order, legacy internal values, or another internal process.

C-17 [Boundary] Reproducibility range of the Pink Turnip bias
Needed comparisons include independent replication with identical conditions, material substitution, changing Pink Turnip's input position, and an equivalent RF5 condition.

C-18 [Derived] Whether subset weights can be distinguished from individual-candidate weights
The 42/50 outcome could arise from a low Pink Turnip weight, weights assigned to whole combinations, staged selection, fixed slots, or generation order.

C-19 [Derived] Conditions under which the model's uniform-selection formula applies
The 4,000 Messhilite trials were consistent with the approximation, while the Pink Turnip condition showed strong bias. Applicability must therefore account for game, candidate composition, material-specific values, and generation path, not only N.

Primary evidence: PDFs 03, 06, 07 §§7–8, 11 §§11-1–11-3, and 12 §§12-1–12-4; RF4SP overflow and weighted-probability materials in the Google Drive “Unresolved Questions” folder

D. Candidate-Count Boundaries and Fixed-Like Behavior
======================================================================

D-01 [Explicit] Whether a real boundary exists near N=3
Results tend to be fixed-like at N≤3 and more competitive or unordered at N≥4, but this is not “always fixed at N=3, always broken at N=4.”

D-02 [Explicit] Whether the boundary depends on total candidates, candidate categories, or vacant slots
The occupancy of the three existing slots or the number of vacancies may matter more than simple N.

D-03 [Explicit] Whether boundary behavior is the same in RF4SP and RF5
RF4SP appears random earlier, while RF5 appears fixed-like at N≤3; the difference is provisional.

D-04 [Explicit] What changes when the boundary is crossed
It is unknown whether candidate generation, final selection, display order, redraw, or displacement changes.

D-05 [Boundary] Why cases such as the Gold Bracelet change less than expected as N rises
Priority, fixed slots, compression, and experimental setup have not been distinguished.

D-06 [Derived] Whether boundaries differ by equipment category or recipe
Observations centered on shoes and accessories cannot yet be generalized to weapons, armor, and shields.

Primary evidence: PDFs 03, 04, 06, 11 §11-5, and 12 §§12-1 and 12-3

E. The Permutation Problem, Placement Order, and Object X
======================================================================

E-01 [Explicit] Whether candidate selection and placement order are separate stages
Order may be determined again after the intended three candidates have been selected.

E-02 [Explicit] Whether all permutations of the final three slots are equally probable
Combination probability and order probability can be multiplied as an approximation, but uniform permutations have not been proven.

E-03 [Explicit] Whether the system shuffles after selection or performs ordered selection
The same display can result from different internal models, which current observations cannot distinguish.

E-04 [Explicit] Whether input, recipe, or source-internal order affects final placement
Fixed and variable order coexist; which ordering information survives is unknown.

E-05 [Explicit] Success probability of placing Object X first
Obtaining the right candidates and placing Object X first are separate conditions; candidate-selection probability alone is insufficient.

E-06 [Explicit] Whether Object X has a special influence on order
It may share ordinary placement weights or have priority or constraints associated with its reversal effect.

E-07 [Explicit] Reproducibility of the roughly 54% Mealy Apple-first bias
Observed in 300 RF4SP trials. Its difference from 50%, condition dependence, and replication remain open.

E-08 [Explicit] Whether the order bias is unique to Mealy Apple
It may apply to all negative-stat materials, to a material category, or only to this material.

E-09 [Explicit] Order distributions for other negative-stat materials
Equivalent comparisons with Heavy Spice, Sour Drop, Mixed Herbs, Sweet Powder, and others have not been performed.

E-10 [Explicit] Completeness of the negative-stat material list
Other materials relevant to Object X reversal may exist.

E-11 [Explicit] Relationship between order bias and overflow redraw or reconstruction
It is unknown whether the same order rule applies at low N and at N≥4.

E-12 [Derived] A general success-probability formula incorporating order
A model combining combination probability, post-selection permutations, and candidate-specific placement weights is incomplete.

E-13 [Explicit] Whether Pink Turnip selection bias and placement-order bias are the same phenomenon
The condition raises both subset-selection and permutation questions. It is unknown whether Pink Turnip is selected less often, placed later after selection, or both.

E-14 [Derived] Whether selection and placement use a common internal priority
A material-specific value or generation order may affect both stages. Separate weights and reuse of one common value have not been distinguished.

Primary evidence: PDFs 06, 07, 08 §8-2, and 12 §12-4; 99_補遺_追加未解決事項備忘録.txt; RF4SP overflow and permutation-test materials in the Google Drive “Unresolved Questions” folder

F. Auto-Arrange
======================================================================

F-01 [Explicit] Whether the general n−3 trigger rule holds universally
Four required materials imply one shortage and five imply two, but this has not been guaranteed across all equipment and games.

F-02 [Explicit] Whether selection from all required materials is uniform
Selection from beginning, middle, and end positions has been observed, but individual rates and weights are unknown.

F-03 [Explicit] Whether a required material's position affects selection rate
The fixed-last-position theory is disfavored, but complete position independence remains unproven.

F-04 [Explicit] Displacement rule when existing arrangements are present
Vacancy filling, low priority, and candidate exclusion remain competing explanations; trials are insufficient.

F-05 [Explicit] Storage format of auto-arrange information
It may be retained internally, but whether material name, order, or generation path is stored is unknown.

F-06 [Explicit] Conditions for recursively expanding stored auto-arrange information
The range in which it carries forward as “candidate-count debt” has not been tested.

F-07 [Explicit] Difference in randomness between RF4SP and RF5
Large controlled comparisons are lacking for the tendency of RF4SP to randomize immediately and RF5 to depend on a boundary.

F-08 [Explicit] Priority relationship between auto-arrange and source contamination
When they compete, which survives more often—and whether this is equipment-dependent—is unknown.

F-09 [Derived] Process conditions that completely avoid auto-arrange
It appears difficult to avoid in high-difficulty equipment, but the optimal process search is incomplete.

Primary evidence: PDFs 03, 05, 06, and 11; Auto-Arrange.md; additional unresolved-question memorandum

======================================================================
G. Source Contamination (formerly “Self-Contamination”)
======================================================================

G-01 [Explicit] Formal unit and name of the phenomenon
“Source contamination” may better describe the observation than “self-contamination.” Final terminology depends on defining what enters the pool.

G-02 [Explicit] Affected equipment categories
The effect is strongly observed in shoes and accessories; equivalent processing in weapons and other categories is unknown.

G-03 [Explicit] Difference in contamination rate between RF4SP and RF5
RF5 appears higher, but controlled comparisons are insufficient.

G-04 [Explicit] Simultaneous candidacy of source equipment and its internal arrangements
It is unknown whether both become candidates or only one does depending on conditions.

G-05 [Explicit] Relationship between inheritance generation, contamination rate, and candidate count
Both tend to rise with repeated inheritance, but linear growth, saturation, and truncation remain possible.

G-06 [Explicit] Relationship with auto-arrange
It is unknown whether source contamination displaces auto-arranged candidates or competes in the same pool.

G-07 [Explicit] Conceptual boundary with recursive processing
A method for separating renewed inclusion of source equipment from renewed expansion of its internal arrangements is incomplete.

G-08 [Derived] Actual probability of the source-removal reroll
The five-candidate RF5 high-difficulty shoe case is illustrative; its candidate generation and probability remain untested.

Primary evidence: PDFs 04, 05, and 11; Self-Contamination.md; additional unresolved-question memorandum

======================================================================
H. Recursive Processing and Generational Retention
======================================================================

H-01 [Explicit] Exact conditions under which recursive processing exists
It is a hypothesis consistent with observations, but expansion is not uniform across all inheritance operations.

H-02 [Explicit] Targets of recursive reference
It is not fully known whether source equipment, additional equipment, auto-arranged information, contamination-derived information, and inherited information are referenced.

H-03 [Explicit] Whether recursion depends on position or role
The source side appears easier to reference, but slot position and processing category have not been distinguished.

H-04 [Explicit] Maximum recursion depth
The number of generations whose internal information can be referenced is untested.

H-05 [Explicit] Maximum number of expanded candidates
It is unknown whether deeply nested structures are fully expanded or stopped at a limit.

H-06 [Explicit] Generation limit and truncation rule
Possible rules include dropping older generations, rejecting newer ones, or choosing by priority.

H-07 [Explicit] Conditions for compression or loss of internal information
Unexpected retention and disappearance partway through inheritance lack a unified explanation.

H-08 [Explicit] Difference in recursion depth between RF4SP and RF5
The current wording remains provisional: RF4SP may be somewhat deeper and RF5 comparatively shallow.

H-09 [Explicit] Whether order information is inherited during recursive expansion
Internal order may be preserved or redetermined along with candidate content.

H-10 [Derived] Prevention of circular reference or repeated expansion
An internal rule must prevent unlimited multiplication when the same origin reappears across generations, but it has not been observed.

H-11 [Derived] Distinguishing recursive processing from candidate compression
Invisible deep candidates may never expand, or may expand and then be compressed or excluded.

Primary evidence: PDFs 05 and 11 §11-4; Recursive-Processing.md; additional unresolved-question memorandum

I. Messhilite Inheritance and the Success-Probability Model
======================================================================

I-01 [Boundary] Generalizability of the 4,000-trial results
How far RF5 results for three through six copies under specific materials, equipment, and procedures extend to other equipment, RF4SP, and other materials is unknown.

I-02 [Explicit] Internal mechanism of same-type population manipulation
The approximation in which duplicate inputs increase the favorable-combination count A is consistent, but internal identification of same-type instances is unknown.

I-03 [Explicit] Large-scale measurement of all-distinct and mixed duplicate-heavy compositions
The theory is organized, but equally extensive trials are lacking.

I-04 [Explicit] Order-dependent success probability involving Object X
A unified model requiring both candidate inclusion and first placement is incomplete.

I-05 [Explicit] Stat-loss rule when Messhilite is included
One intended material slot likely loses its effect, but this is not guaranteed for every material and condition.

I-06 [Explicit] Effect of fixed-like behavior, priority, and redraw on theoretical values
Conditions that depart from the uniform-combination model have not been quantified.

I-07 [On Hold] ATK +22 versus +52 in the six-Glitta Augite setup
Iron versus Dragonic Stone and direct versus inherited crafting conditions are mixed, leaving the cause unresolved.

I-08 [On Hold] Additional ATK +10 and M.ATK +5
It matches the total item-level bonus, but does not occur every time with the same composition; relations to material and item levels are untested.

I-09 [Explicit] Separating contributions of Iron, Dragonic Stone, base weapon, source equipment, and input count
No controlled experiment has isolated the condition behind I-07.

I-10 [On Hold] Input-order records for past observations
Records do not establish whether the order was Glitta→Glitta→Messhilite or Messhilite→Glitta→Glitta.

I-11 [Derived] Separating the success-rate model from the stat-value model
If stat application is a separate stage after successful candidate selection, candidate count alone cannot predict final stats.

Primary evidence: PDFs 07 and 08; research PDFs and 07_統合データ.csv; Success-Probability.md; additional unresolved-question memorandum

J. Differences Between Games, Versions, and Environments
======================================================================

J-01 [Explicit] Differences between original RF4 and RF4SP
Internal behavioral changes from porting or fixes have not been compared.

J-02 [Explicit] Candidate-count boundary differences between RF4SP and RF5
Only limited comparisons exist; large controlled trials are needed.

J-03 [Explicit] Differences in fixed-like behavior
The provisional characterization—stronger in RF4SP and strongest at N≤3 in RF5—requires confirmation.

J-04 [Explicit] Different onset points for unordered behavior
RF5 may change markedly at N≥4, while RF4SP may vary earlier.

J-05 [Explicit] Difference in source-contamination rate
RF5 appears higher, but evidence is insufficient.

J-06 [Explicit] Difference in auto-arrange priority
The interpretation that RF5 has stronger effects or more vacancy-filling behavior requires testing.

J-07 [Explicit] Differences in compression, internal priority, and weights
All remain hypothetical; the games may use different selection structures.

J-08 [Explicit] Difference in recursion depth
No cross-game controlled comparison has been performed.

J-09 [Explicit] Game-specific effect of inheritance order
The RF4SP effect is unknown; an RF5 effect remains hypothetical.

J-10 [Explicit] Hardware, patch, and port differences
Reproducibility across Steam, Switch, versions, and presumed latest-patch environments is not organized.

J-11 [Derived] Generalization to RF3 and other titles
It is untested whether the same Candidate Count structure applies, including to material-shop selection.

Primary evidence: PDFs 03–06, 11 §11-5, and 12; English-language articles

K. Optimization of High-Difficulty Inheritance Processes
======================================================================

K-01 [Explicit] Optimal inheritance path to the same target
Combinations of process splitting, intermediate equipment, and candidate-count management remain unexplored.

K-02 [Explicit] Definition of the optimization objective
Expected attempts, material consumption, verification work, reloads, and mental burden conflict; there is no unique optimum.

K-03 [Explicit] Whether special-ability compression truly reduces candidate count
If intermediate equipment is recursively expanded, candidates may proliferate again after compression.

K-04 [Explicit] Tradeoff between avoiding auto-arrange and avoiding source contamination
The hypothesis that complete avoidance is impossible has not been proven or falsified by equipment type.

K-05 [Explicit] Whether the 25% Ghost/Rocket compatibility reroll is real
It assumes four candidates; the actual candidate set has not been directly verified.

K-06 [Explicit] Source-removal reroll for RF5 Fairy Boots stat transfer
The generation process and actual probability in the five-candidate example are untested.

K-07 [Explicit] Minimum number of 25% rerolls
Reducing three to one appears difficult, while three to two is a provisional estimate; exhaustive process search is incomplete.

K-08 [Explicit] Actual workload reduction from split saves
The operational hypothesis reduces burden by separating independent stages without raising success probability, but lacks quantitative comparison.

K-09 [Derived] Boundary between theoretically strongest and practically strongest
The exchange rate between one additional effect and expected attempts, materials, and time is undefined.

K-10 [Derived] Process-optimization algorithm
An exact path-search model cannot yet be built while generation, recursion, weighting, and order remain unresolved.

Primary evidence: PDFs 09 and 11 §11-6; 99_補遺_追加未解決事項備忘録.txt

L-01 [Explicit] Whether shop and trade-good selection is weighted
Generalization to Heinz (RF5), Leon (RF4SP), Raven (RF4SP), Sakuya (RF3), Terry rerolls, and similar systems remains hypothetical.

L-02 [Explicit] Relationship between shipped-item count and shop appearance rate
Quantitative verification is lacking for the dilution hypothesis: expanding the pool makes a specific item less likely to appear.

L-03 [Explicit] Whether candidate pools are shared or separated by shop category

L-04 [Explicit] Duplicate rules and selection method for shop slots
Without-replacement, weighted, and staged selection remain possible internal rules.

L-05 [Derived] Whether inheritance and shop candidate-count problems use the same model
They are structurally similar, but there is no basis yet for assuming the same formula and selection constraints.

M. Observation Design, Statistics, and Reproducibility
======================================================================

M-01 [Explicit] Trial counts required to detect low-frequency bias
No power analysis exists for weighted selection, order bias, or cross-game differences.

M-02 [Explicit] Insufficient condition control
Replications simultaneously fixing equipment, material level, item level, input order, source, internal arrangements, and patch environment are lacking.

M-03 [Derived] How to infer unobservable internal candidates
Completed outcomes cannot uniquely reconstruct generation, selection, and order; this is an identifiability problem.

M-04 [Derived] Minimum experiment that distinguishes competing hypotheses
A controlled design separating uniform selection, weighting, fixed slots, compression, and staged selection is incomplete.

M-05 [Explicit] Missing historical observation records
Some input orders and conditions depend on memory and cannot be reanalyzed.

M-06 [Boundary] Meaning of agreement within a 95% confidence interval
It shows no conflict with the model, but does not exclude competing models that predict the same values.

M-07 [Derived] Multiple comparisons and exploratory observation
Management of chance bias when exploring many materials, orders, and game differences is unresolved.

M-08 [Derived] Scope of raw-data publication
The 4,000-trial CSV is a strong foundation, but full raw logs for the 300 order-bias trials, boundary tests, and other observations may not exist at the same level.

M-09 [Explicit] Replication design for the 50 Pink Turnip trials
Moving from detecting bias to identifying its cause requires controlled material substitution, input-position swaps, generation-order swaps, fixed N, and fixed equipment and recipe.

M-10 [Derived] Interpretation of a bias discovered post hoc
Because 20 trials were added after the initial 30 showed bias, reproducibility should also be evaluated on the 20 confirmatory trials separately from the full 50-trial test.

Primary evidence: research PDFs and 07_統合データ.csv; PDFs 11 and 12; additional unresolved-question memorandum; research spreadsheets in the Google Drive “Unresolved Questions” folder

======================================================================
Core Research Bundles (Dependencies, Not Priority Ranking)
======================================================================

Bundle 1: Defining candidate count
Until A-01 through A-10 are resolved, the meaning of N may vary by condition.

Bundle 2: Uniform selection vs. weighting, fixed slots, compression, or staged selection
C-01 through C-19 are an identifiability problem among competing models that explain the same observations. The 50 Pink Turnip trials provide a concrete condition under which uniform selection cannot be assumed universally.

Bundle 3: Separating combinations from permutations
E-01 through E-12 require separate treatment of the probability of obtaining the right candidates and the probability of placing them in the right order.

Bundle 4: Boundary between recursion and source contamination
Sections G and H require identifying from outcomes which candidates were added through which path.

Bundle 5: Differences between games
Section J requires separating a shared core that permits RF4SP and RF5 to use one theory from game-specific layers.

Bundle 6: From model consistency to mechanism identification
The 4,000 trials support consistency with the model. The next stage is observation design that distinguishes mechanisms predicting the same success rate.
