Candidate Count Model Research: Current Unresolved Questions and Public Frontier
Rune-Factory-Inheritance-Research

Created: 2026-08-01
Current Frontier revision: 2026-08-16

Purpose
-------
This document is the current public view of the unresolved questions that remain around the Candidate Count Model, organized so that another researcher can resume from observable in-game reality.

The 2026-08-01 edition collected 134 questions across the repository. That historical set remains useful for traceability, but not every historical question is a current Public Frontier question.

The current classification separates:
- Public Frontier: questions that can lead from observable gameplay reality to another observation or test
- Supporting Evidence / Example: known reality that supports a frontier question
- Internal / Identifiability Hold: implementation questions that current observations cannot uniquely distinguish
- Closed / Current Boundary: questions reduced to an established observation or present boundary
- Method / Evidence Policy: research-design and evidence-management questions
- Practical / Optimization: operational optimization questions
- Adjacent Frontier: important questions outside the central Candidate Count mission

Reference-assisted findings are not imported backward as public answers. The public frontier is based on the reality and unknowns reachable from actual gameplay observation.

Research Principles
-------------------
Reality First.

- The Candidate Count Model is a replaceable approximation for organizing gameplay observations, not proof of internal code.
- Agreement between a model and observations does not eliminate competing internal mechanisms that make the same predictions.
- The priority is to identify where a simple approximation works and where it stops working, rather than to guess the name of an internal implementation.
- Reference-assisted findings are not automatically transferred into the Public Frontier.
- A question remaining open does not mean the project must continue investing in it.

What Actual Gameplay Has Established So Far
-------------------------------------------
1. Candidate Count is a useful approximation axis for reasoning about inheritance success rates.
2. In 4,000 RF5 Messhilite trials across three- through six-stack conditions, strong consistency with the Candidate Count approximation was observed.
3. However, conditions exist where the same Candidate Count produces different outcome distributions depending on candidate composition.
4. In an RF4SP overflow condition, combinations excluding Pink Turnip occurred in 42 of 50 trials, reproducing a bias difficult to explain with a simple equal-probability approximation.
5. Ordering of the final three candidates is not necessarily uniform across all candidate sets.
6. Candidate Count alone therefore cannot always predict selection, ordering, and final performance with one simple formula.
7. The internal cause of these biases has not been uniquely identified from gameplay observations.

CURRENT PUBLIC FRONTIER — CORE
======================================================================

PF-01  Boundary of the Uniform Approximation
--------------------------------------------
Under what conditions can candidates with the same Candidate Count be treated as approximately uniform, and where does that approximation break?

Known reality:
- Some conditions agree well with the Candidate Count approximation.
- Other conditions with the same Candidate Count show strong bias.

Still unknown:
- What candidate compositions make the uniform approximation useful
- What observable boundary predicts its breakdown
- When Candidate Count alone is sufficient and when additional measurement is needed

This is not a rejection of the Candidate Count Model. It is a question about its safe range of application.


PF-02  Candidate Groups / Properties and Bias
---------------------------------------------
Do candidates have some still-unidentified grouping or observable property that is related to selection or Ordering bias?

Known reality:
- Different materials show different selection and Ordering tendencies.
- Some candidate sets appear comparatively even.
- Conditions containing Pink Turnip have shown strong bias.

Still unknown:
- Whether candidates with similar uses or types tend to become more even
- Whether bias is item-specific or combination-dependent
- Whether the boundary can be predicted from features visible to a player

“Group” here does not mean a field from internal reference data. It refers to classifications that can be proposed from gameplay-observable properties.


PF-03  Relationship Between Selection and Ordering
--------------------------------------------------
Is being selected more often the same phenomenon as being placed earlier after selection, or are these separate observable effects?

Known reality:
- Some conditions show non-uniform candidate-subset selection.
- Some conditions show non-uniform Ordering among the final three candidates.

Still unknown:
- Whether Selection and Ordering respond to the same candidate properties
- Whether conditions exist where only one is biased
- How both change across Candidate Count boundaries

This does not assert a particular number of internal processing stages. It asks whether two observable realities move together.


PF-04  What the Candidate Count Boundary Represents
---------------------------------------------------
When behavior changes sharply, which observable variable best describes the boundary: total candidate count, vacant slots, candidate composition, generation path, or something else?

Known reality:
- The region around N≈3/4 is a useful practical boundary indicator.
- It is not a universal rule that N=3 is always fixed and N=4 always breaks.

Still unknown:
- The main observable conditions that move the boundary
- Differences by equipment category or recipe
- Whether Candidate Count needs another simple companion indicator


PF-05  Range of Auto Arrange
----------------------------
Under what conditions does Auto Arrange enter candidate competition, and how generally can its selection or displacement behavior be described?

Known reality:
- Auto Arrange can occur when recipes require many materials.
- Materials from early, middle, and late recipe positions can appear.
- Competition with existing candidates cannot be explained by a simple fixed-position rule alone.

Still unknown:
- The general range of the n−3 approximation
- Where uniform approximation among required materials is useful
- Observable boundaries when Auto Arrange competes with existing arrangements or source contamination


PF-06  Boundary of Source Contamination and Recursive Inheritance
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Under what conditions do source contamination and recursive inheritance increase candidate pressure, and how far back can their effects remain observable?

Known reality:
- Source-derived information affects outcomes under some conditions.
- Information from earlier inheritance generations can affect later results.
- Not every path expands in the same way.

Still unknown:
- Trigger conditions and equipment-category differences
- How many generations retain observable influence
- Whether candidate content, Ordering, or both can transport

Exact internal storage formats and recursive algorithms remain separate implementation questions.


PF-07  Transport Between RF4SP and RF5
--------------------------------------
Which Candidate Count, Ordering, and Auto Arrange boundaries observed in RF4SP also hold in RF5?

Known reality:
- Some phenomena appear in both games.
- Differences are suggested in fixed-like behavior, disordering, and source contamination.

Still unknown:
- Which boundaries transport between games
- Where game-specific behavior begins
- When the same simple approximation is safe in both games


PF-08  Boundary Between Simple Prediction and Individual Measurement
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Can actual gameplay observations tell us in advance when Candidate Count alone is enough and when candidate-specific bias or Ordering must be measured separately?

This is the central question that connects the current Public Frontier.

The goal is not complete reconstruction of the internal implementation.

A useful outcome would be the ability to distinguish:
- conditions where a simple approximation is sufficient
- conditions that require caution
- conditions that require individual measurement

That would make the Candidate Count Model safer and more useful without turning it into an internal-spec claim.


======================================================================
ADJACENT FRONTIER
======================================================================

PF-A1  Separating Success Probability from Final Performance Values
-------------------------------------------------------------------
After the intended inheritance candidates succeed, what determines the final performance values?

Candidate Count mainly addresses the probability that desired candidates survive. Final stat determination may be a separate problem involving materials, base equipment, source equipment, input count, and other factors.

This remains important, but is adjacent to the central Candidate Count mission.


PF-A2  Generalization to Other Candidate-Pool Systems
-----------------------------------------------------
Can appearance bias in shops or trade inventories be understood using the same Candidate Count idea as equipment inheritance?

There are structural similarities involving candidate pools, visible slots, duplication, and appearance rates. There is not yet evidence that the same formula or selection structure applies.

This is retained as an adjacent frontier rather than a core claim.


======================================================================
QUESTIONS REMOVED FROM THE PUBLIC FRONTIER
======================================================================

1. Internal / Identifiability Hold
----------------------------------
Examples:
- whether an internal priority exists
- whether fixed slots exist
- whether selection occurs in stages
- whether a shuffle occurs after selection
- the internal RNG seed
- the internal storage format
- whether one common priority parameter exists

These are interesting questions, but multiple internal mechanisms can produce the same observed gameplay result. Until competing explanations generate different observable predictions, they are not central Public Frontier questions.


2. Supporting Evidence / Example
--------------------------------
Individual observed phenomena are retained as evidence rather than multiplied into separate active questions.

Examples include:
- the 50-trial Pink Turnip observation
- non-uniform Ordering
- non-fixed Auto Arrange positions
- individual RF4SP/RF5 differences


3. Method / Evidence Policy
---------------------------
Required sample size, condition control, raw-data publication, multiple comparisons, and missing historical records remain important.

They are research-method questions rather than unresolved game-mechanic questions and are managed separately.


4. Practical / Optimization
---------------------------
Optimal inheritance routes, save splitting, expected retry counts, and the boundary between theoretical and practical best equipment are optimization questions rather than the Public Research Frontier.


======================================================================
CURRENT POSITION OF THE PINK TURNIP PROBLEM
======================================================================

In an RF4SP candidate-overflow condition, combinations excluding Pink Turnip occurred in 42 of 50 trials.

That is an important observed reality.

It does not establish that:
- Pink Turnip has a low internal weight
- a particular internal category causes the effect
- an ID or registration order causes the effect
- fixed slots or staged selection exist

The Public Frontier question is:

“Why can a condition containing Pink Turnip show such a large bias even when Candidate Count is unchanged?”

A researcher continuing from gameplay alone might naturally ask:
- what happens with three crop-like candidates
- what happens with seeds
- what happens with ores or another natural group
- what happens when Pink Turnip is replaced
- whether the bias remains when the surrounding candidate set changes

These are examples of questions generated by the current reality, not instructions or claims about an internal cause.

PUBLIC EVIDENCE BOUNDARY
======================================================================

The Current Public Frontier is based on questions that were reachable from actual gameplay observation before later reference-assisted analysis.

Answers or candidate explanations obtained from later reference-assisted analysis, internal metadata, or simulations using those materials are not automatically imported backward into the Public Frontier.

This does not invalidate those internal research records.

The public path remains:

Reality
↓
Unknown
↓
Next Reality

The priority is to leave a path by which another researcher can reach the same frontier from observable reality, rather than to publish an answer that cannot be reconstructed from the public evidence chain.


======================================================================
CURRENT SUMMARY
======================================================================

The Candidate Count Model remains a useful approximation for reasoning about candidate space and success probability.

Actual gameplay observations also show that:

same Candidate Count
≠
same outcome distribution

three candidates survive
≠
all Orderings are always equally probable

The central problem is therefore not to name the complete internal implementation.

It is to identify, from reality:

“Under what conditions is a simple approximation useful, and where does it stop being useful?”
