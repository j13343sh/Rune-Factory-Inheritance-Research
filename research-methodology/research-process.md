# Research Process

![Research Process](../images/research-methodology/asset-01-research-process-en.png)

## Purpose

This diagram presents research not as a simple linear procedure, but as a cycle of defining a question, evaluating multiple perspectives in parallel, making a decision, taking action, learning, and observing again.

## Key Ideas

- Research begins by defining a question.
- Multiple perspectives are evaluated in parallel.
- Evidence, feasibility, cost, alternatives, and benefits are considered together.
- A decision leads to action and Validation.
- Results are analyzed and generate the next question.
- A Model is a temporary tool for approaching Reality.

## Repository Context

This approach has informed boundary tests for candidate counts, Messhilite validation, proxy observation design, and searches for Counterexamples. These applications differ in detail, but they share an emphasis on explicit questions, observable evidence, parallel evaluation, and renewed Validation.

## Limitations

This diagram is a conceptual summary.

It does not represent every experiment as a fixed sequence. Actual research may return to earlier stages whenever new evidence appears.
